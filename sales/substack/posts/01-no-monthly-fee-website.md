# How to give a small business a real website without a monthly fee

*Subtitle: Most shops don't need a website builder. They need one good page, a domain, and ten minutes.*

Plenty of cafés pay around $29 a month for a website builder. Over three years that's more than $1,000, for a site with a menu, opening hours, a map and a phone number, which often hasn't changed since the day it went live.

That's the most common small-business website there is, and it doesn't need a subscription.

## What a small business site actually needs

Look at what customers do on a local business site. They check:

1. Are you open right now?
2. Where are you, and how do I get there?
3. What do you sell, and roughly what does it cost?
4. How do I call, message or book?

That's it. Four questions, one page. Everything else (blog, member area, online store) is optional, and most shops never use it.

## The no-subscription setup

**1. One HTML file.** A single page that answers those four questions, works on phones, and loads fast. You can write it yourself, hire someone once, or start from a template.

**2. Free hosting.** Netlify, Cloudflare Pages and GitHub Pages all host a static site like this for free, with HTTPS included. On Netlify you can literally drag the folder onto a web page and it's live.

**3. Your own domain.** About $10–15 a year from Cloudflare, Namecheap or Porkbun. This is the only recurring cost, and you'd pay it with a website builder too.

**4. Google Business Profile.** Free, and for local shops it matters more than the website itself. Add your website link there.

Total yearly cost: the domain. That's it.

## What you give up

Being honest about the trade-offs:

- **No drag-and-drop editor.** Changing your hours means opening a text file and editing one line. If that sounds scary, it's genuinely easier than it sounds: search for the old text, type the new text, save.
- **No built-in online ordering.** You can still link to Square, Toast, Uber Eats or a booking tool. Most shops already use one of those anyway.
- **You're responsible for it.** No support chat. In practice, a static page has almost nothing that can break.

## When a website builder *is* worth it

If you sell hundreds of products online, need customer accounts, or update the site every day, a platform like Shopify or Squarespace earns its fee. For a restaurant, salon, clinic or studio that just needs to be found and contacted, it usually doesn't.
