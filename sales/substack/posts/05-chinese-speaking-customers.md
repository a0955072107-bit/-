# Your customers read Chinese. Does your website?

*Subtitle: Why a shop in Flushing, Arcadia or San Gabriel should have its website in English, 繁體 and 简体, and what it takes.*

Walk into a dental office in the San Gabriel Valley and the front desk will often greet you in English, Mandarin and Cantonese. The menu at a dumpling house in Queens lists every dish in English and Chinese. The shops already serve customers in their own language.

Then look at their websites. Most are in English only.

## Who is reading your site

More than three million people in the United States speak Chinese at home, according to the Census Bureau's American Community Survey. Many of them are perfectly comfortable in English. Many are not, especially older relatives, recent arrivals and parents choosing a tutor or a dentist for their kids.

For those visitors, an English-only site creates small frictions:

- They can't tell if you take their insurance, so they call, or don't.
- They aren't sure about the hours, so they check another shop.
- They can't share the page with a parent who reads only Chinese.

None of this shows up in your analytics. People just leave.

## Traditional and Simplified are not the same audience

Chinese-speaking customers in the US come from Taiwan, Hong Kong, mainland China, Malaysia and Singapore. Customers from Taiwan and Hong Kong usually read Traditional characters (繁體). Customers from mainland China usually read Simplified (简体). Either one can read the other with some effort, but each feels more at home in their own.

So a good Chinese version is really two versions. That sounds like double the work, but it isn't: you write one, convert it with a tool like OpenCC, and fix a handful of words by hand.

## What a trilingual site needs

- **A language button that's easy to find.** Top corner, one tap, no hunting.
- **Each language as a full page,** so Google can show the Chinese page to someone searching in Chinese.
- **The details that matter most in every language:** hours, address, phone, and the one or two things people ask about most (insurance, pricing, booking).
- **Names that stay recognisable.** Keep your English business name next to your Chinese one, so customers can match the sign on the street to the page on their phone.
