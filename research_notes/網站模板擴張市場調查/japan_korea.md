# Japan & Korea Market Entry for One-Page HTML Templates + Website-Building Services (Solo Seller in Taiwan)

> Method note: page fetching (WebFetch) was blocked in this research session for every domain tried (nta.go.jp, kmong.com, forvismazars.com, atsoho.com). All findings below come from search-engine result snippets and summaries of the cited URLs, not from full-page reads. Treat numbers as "reported by the cited page". Re-verify anything decision-critical on the original page. USD conversions use rough rates of ~150 JPY/USD and ~1,400 KRW/USD (my assumption, not sourced). Research date: 2026-11-22.

## 1. Demand evidence: how JP/KR small businesses get websites

### Takeaway
Japan: SMBs still use standalone websites heavily, and there is a big DIY-builder plus cheap-freelancer ecosystem (ペライチ, Jimdo, STUDIO, Wix, Coconala LP sellers at ¥15k–50k). Korea: small shops rely mainly on Naver Place, Instagram, KakaoTalk channels and Danggeun. Standalone sites are secondary, but the June 2025 shutdown of Naver modoo pushed "hundreds of thousands" of small sites toward imweb and similar builders. That creates a one-time migration wave, not obviously a market for pure-HTML files.

### Cited Findings
**Japan**
- An old Tokyo Chamber of Commerce survey (FY2011, **flag: very outdated**) found about 84% of firms with 10 or fewer employees had a website. No 2025 website-ownership rate turned up — [MoneyForward](https://biz.moneyforward.com/blog/25881/)
- In a JFC survey (Jan–Mar 2025), 43.6% of SMEs were "active" on digitalization, and about 70% said they had digitalized more than five years earlier (survey Mar 2024) — [JFC 2025](https://www.jfc.go.jp/n/findings/pdf/tokubetu_250521.pdf); [JFC topics 181](https://www.jfc.go.jp/n/findings/pdf/topics_no181_2409_1.pdf)
- ペライチ (a Japanese one-page builder, closest in concept to the seller's product) reportedly ended its free tier in Oct 2025. Its Light plan is ¥1,480/month (¥1,628 incl. tax), up to 3 pages, with payment fees from 4.5% (6.0% on Light) — [Lancers comparison](https://www.lancers.jp/c/website/website-knowhow/2851/) / [atsoho compare](https://atsoho.com/apps/compare/jimdo-vs-peraichi) (secondary comparison sites, 2026)
- Jimdo: Free ¥0, Start ¥1,290/month, Grow ¥2,070/month (annual billing). Free plans on Wix, Jimdo and STUDIO show ads and cannot use a custom domain — same comparison sources as above
- Coconala lists many LP/HP creation gigs from roughly ¥15,000 to ¥220,000. Coconala's own magazine says ¥20,000–50,000 is the most common LP price band — [Coconala magazine](https://coconala.com/magazine/151); [example ¥15k LP gig](https://coconala.com/services/4307195)
- Coconala already carries template products, e.g. Canva LP templates for salons and seminars (about ¥3,000) and "HTML template sales" for creators' portfolios — [salon LP template](https://coconala.com/services/4240007); [seminar LP template](https://coconala.com/services/4274263); [HTML template](https://coconala.com/services/4268405)
- BOOTH has a one-page HTML template for personal gyms (HTML/CSS plus a dist folder, with "human-made" and "AI-assisted" versions). The price wasn't visible in the snippet — [BOOTH item](https://booth.pm/en/items/8835446)
- LINE reached 100M monthly users in Japan at end of Dec 2025 (99M in mid-2025). It is the default customer-contact channel — [LY Corp](https://www.lycorp.co.jp/ja/story/20260210/line_100million.html); [Impress Watch](https://www.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/2081911.html)

**Korea**
- Naver announced on 2024-12-26 that its free SMB homepage builder **modoo!** would close, with a final shutdown on **2025-06-26**. After the announcement, new links to Place, reservations and SmartStore were no longer possible. An industry insider estimated the users looking for alternatives at "hundreds of thousands" — [Daum/news](https://v.daum.net/v/20250610165902945?f=p); [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202506105746g)
- imweb launched a "modoo → imweb easy migration" service in Feb 2025: upload the backup and migration finishes in about 3 minutes on average, free for imweb users. Gabia's Clicken pitched a migration with Naver TalkTalk, Naver Pay and Naver Map integration — [Byline Network](https://byline.network/2025/02/27-408/); [News1](https://www.news1.kr/amp/industry/sb-founded/5703732); [imweb blog](https://imweb.me/blog?idx=359)
- A Naver-commissioned study (Aug–Sep 2025, 3,257 small sellers with revenue ≤ ₩100M) found Place users rate **maps/directions** as the most important feature, and non-capital-region businesses rate **online reservations** higher. **Bias flag: Naver-funded** — [Segye](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20251119514916); [Naver](https://navercorp.com/media/naverReportsDetail?seq=33572)
- Toss Place's guide for small merchants frames their channel mix as Naver Place, Instagram, Danggeun business profile and blog, and notes owners "don't have time to run them all". A standalone homepage is not a core channel in this framing — [Toss Place](https://tossplace.com/story/online_marketing_channels)
- Naver Place added AI Briefing (review summaries) in June 2025, which raised the importance of review management — [i-boss](https://www.i-boss.co.kr/ab-6141-70824)
- KakaoTalk had about 46.6M–48.0M MAU in autumn 2025 (MobileIndex vs WiseApp; Kakao disputed the third-party figures) — [Segye](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20251112512845); [THE FACT](https://news.tf.co.kr/read/economy/2264937.htm)
- Kmong already sells imweb templates bundled with e-books (e.g. "16 imweb templates + 2 pricing sections + e-book"), delivered by transferring template ownership to the buyer's imweb account. One listing bundles **5 HTML templates (cafe/clinic/portfolio/shop/corporate)**. Prices were not visible — [Kmong gig 809166](https://kmong.com/gig/809166); [Kmong gig 654095](https://kmong.com/gig/654095); [Kmong gig 760139](https://kmong.com/gig/760139)

### Inferences
- In Japan, the buyer for a pure-HTML file is mostly a **web freelancer, designer or developer** (people who sell ¥15k–50k LPs on Coconala), not the shop owner. Shop owners there pick ペライチ/Jimdo/STUDIO or hire someone. A Japanese "template for freelancers to resell" angle (commercial-use license, ¥3,000–10,000) looks more realistic than selling to shop owners directly.
- In Korea, the template market is tied to **imweb**. Products sold there are imweb templates, not raw HTML. A raw HTML one-pager has little pull with Korean shop owners, who need Naver Place/Booking/TalkTalk/Kakao integration rather than a static site. The modoo shutdown created a 2025 migration spike, but that window has largely passed (Nov 2026).
- Language switchers (EN/繁/简/JA/KO) matter most for **inbound-tourism businesses** (Japanese B&Bs/ryokan, salons, clinics and restaurants serving Taiwanese/Chinese/Korean/English tourists). This is a differentiated niche the seller's multilingual templates fit well, arguably better than "Japanese-only" versions.

### Gaps
- No search-volume data (Google Keyword Planner, Naver DataLab) for "ホームページ テンプレート", "LP テンプレート" or "홈페이지 템플릿" could be retrieved without page access. Recommend checking Naver DataLab and Google Trends directly.
- No 2025–2026 statistic on JP or KR SMB website ownership was found. Korea's Ministry of SMEs/Startups small-business survey (소상공인실태조사) would be the source to check.
- No market share data for STUDIO, Wix or imweb.

## 2. Platforms: fees, foreign-seller eligibility, payouts

### Takeaway
Japan: **Coconala allows sellers living overseas, but requires a Japanese phone number for SMS, a Japan-issued ID and a Japanese bank account for payouts**, so a Taiwanese individual without a JP bank account is effectively blocked. BOOTH (5.6% + ¥45 since Oct 2025) is the most realistic Japanese storefront for digital files, but its payout options for non-Japan residents could not be verified. Korea: Kmong, Soomgo and Taling almost certainly require Korean identity verification (phone tied to a resident registration number) and a Korean bank account. **No source confirms that a Taiwan-based individual can register and be paid; assume no until proven otherwise.**

### Cited Findings
**Coconala (Japan)**
- Seller fee: flat **22% (incl. tax)** of the sale price, still 22% in 2026. Video-chat services rose to 27.5% from 2025-04-16 — [Coconala news 1165](https://coconala.com/news/1165); [atsoho fee breakdown 2026](https://atsoho.com/blog/coconala-fee-2026-breakdown)
- Official help: **overseas residents can list services, but SMS verification only accepts domestic phone numbers, ID documents must be Japan-issued, and payout by bank transfer requires a Japanese bank account** — [Coconala help: 出品審査・海外からの出品について](https://help.coconala.com/hc/ja/articles/218722417)
- Identity verification has been mandatory for payout requests since 2024-02-16 — [Coconala news 959](https://coconala.com/news/959)
- Bloggers living abroad receive payouts via a Japanese account (or PayPal with double fees). These are Japanese nationals abroad, not foreigners, and **the posts are several years old** — [spain-ryo.net](https://spain-ryo.net/coconalawithdraw/); [penguin-no-te.com](https://penguin-no-te.com/coconala/)
- Competition: an unofficial analysis found about 70% of ¥10k–30k LP listings had zero sales, and only 263 of 655 listings (40.2%) had any review. **Unofficial blog; treat with caution** — cited in [Coconala LP search summary](https://coconala.com/blogs/6165551/788154)

**CrowdWorks / Lancers (Japan)**
- CrowdWorks: workers living overseas can only receive payment into a **domestic Japanese bank account**, and verification for overseas residents is based on a Japan-issued passport (forum quoting the help center) — [CrowdWorks consultation thread](https://crowdworks.jp/consultation/threads/16668)
- Lancers: no foreign-seller policy found (gap).

**BOOTH (pixiv, Japan)**
- Service fee raised from 5.6% + ¥22 to **5.6% + ¥45** on 2025-10-28 (payment processing included) — [GameBusiness.jp 2025-07-24](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2025/07/24/24711.html); [BOOTH announcement](https://booth.pm/announcements/834)
- BOOTH has supported payouts to a **PayPal account (Thailand-registered example)** since March 2022, which suggests PayPal payouts for non-JP residents exist. **Dated 2022; current status unverified** — [BOOTH announcement 562](https://booth.pm/announcements/562)
- An unverified report says BOOTH stopped Wise payouts and moved to bank/PayPal — [quasa.io](https://quasa.io/ja/media/booth-ga-wise-furikomi-o-teishi-11gatsu-uriagekara-ginkoka-paypale)
- Operator: pixiv Inc.; the fee is deducted from sales — [BOOTH terms](https://booth.pm/legal)

**Kmong (Korea)**
- Payouts equal the transaction amount minus service fee, payment fee, network fee and VAT. Enterprise projects take 13.2% (incl. VAT). Standard gig commission rates were not visible in the snippets (gap) — [Kmong FAQ 250](https://kmong.com/support/customer-center/posts/250)
- Individuals can sell before registering a business, but **business registration and a mail-order business report (통신판매업 신고) become mandatory once certain conditions are met**. A new tax-reporting regime applies from Jan 2026 — [Kmong expert center 708](https://kmong.com/support/expert-center/posts/708); [Kmong new seller guide](https://kmong.com/support/expert-center/posts/766)
- Korean mobile identity verification generally needs a number registered in your own name with a resident registration number (RRN), which excludes non-residents — [Asia Society Korea](https://asiasociety.org/korea/mobile-phone-verification-can-i-get-your-number)
- Top tier: Kmong "Prime" sellers (top 2%) offer LP creation. One new LP seller lists a STANDARD package at **₩90,000 launch price (regular ₩190,000)**, with a 1–3 business-day turnaround once materials are ready — [Kmong gig 796249](https://kmong.com/gig/796249); [Kmong gig 772452](https://kmong.com/gig/772452)

**Soomgo (Korea)**
- Free to join. Pros pay to send quotes (charged only when the customer opens the quote; refunded as bonus cash if not opened within 48 hours) — [Soomgo pro page](https://soomgo.com/pro)
- A blog reports ₩10,000–50,000 per quote for categories like website creation, non-refundable when no deal results. **Single blog; verify** — [wikidocs blog](https://wikidocs.net/blog/@knarchive/26221/)
- App reviews say foreign instructors find the app hard and get few replies — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1250771188)

**Taling (Korea)**: no data found (gap).

### Inferences
- **Coconala, CrowdWorks and Kmong are effectively closed** to a Taiwan-resident non-Japanese/non-Korean individual unless they have a JP/KR bank account and phone, and even then Japan-issued or Korean ID is required.
- **Best realistic channels:** (1) keep **Gumroad** as the checkout (merchant of record, see section 5) and add JA/KO landing pages; (2) **BOOTH** for Japanese discovery, if PayPal payout to a Taiwan account is confirmed; (3) for services, direct sales via your own JA/KO pages, LINE Official Account (JP) and KakaoTalk Channel (KR), paid through Gumroad, Stripe or PayPal.
- Fee comparison: Coconala 22% vs BOOTH about 5.6% + ¥45 (about 7.4% on a ¥2,500 item) vs Gumroad (fee not researched here).

### Gaps
- BOOTH's current payout options for non-Japan residents (PayPal to a Taiwan-registered account?) and whether BOOTH requires a Japanese address for the 特商法 disclosure. Check the BOOTH help center.
- Kmong, Soomgo and Taling official policies on foreign non-resident sellers (help-center pages were not readable).
- Kmong's standard commission rate (widely reported elsewhere as tiered, about 20% down to lower rates by amount, but not verified in this session).
- Lancers' foreign-seller rules.

## 3. Typical prices

### Takeaway
Both markets price **custom work far below** the seller's $249–449. Japan: LPs commonly ¥15k–50k (about $100–330), with newcomers as low as ¥3k–8k. Korea: LPs about ₩90k–190k at the entry level (about $65–135), with agency quotes for full sites at ₩3–4M. Templates sell for about ¥3,000 (Canva LP templates on Coconala). The current $39/$199 pricing is high next to Japanese template prices but plausible for a multilingual "commercial-use bundle for freelancers".

### Cited Findings
- Coconala's most common LP band is ¥20,000–50,000. Listings range from about ¥20k to ¥220k — [Coconala magazine 151](https://coconala.com/magazine/151)
- Coconala ¥15,000 LP (3-day delivery, "no extra fees") — [Coconala 4307195](https://coconala.com/services/4307195)
- Coconala HTML/CSS LP for "building a portfolio": Basic ¥3,000 (3 sections), Standard ¥5,000 (5 sections + form), Premium ¥8,000 (7 sections). Delivers HTML/CSS only, with no WordPress and no server upload — [Coconala 4057175](https://coconala.com/services/4057175)
- Coconala Canva LP templates (salon, seminar) shown at ¥3,000, with redistribution prohibited — [Coconala 4240007](https://coconala.com/services/4240007)
- Kmong LP STANDARD at ₩90,000 launch / ₩190,000 regular — [Kmong 796249](https://kmong.com/gig/796249)
- An imweb seller's review mentions other agencies quoting ₩3–4M for a site (anecdotal) — [Kmong 668294](https://kmong.com/gig/668294)
- Monthly builder costs as the alternative: ペライチ ¥1,628, Jimdo ¥1,290–2,070 — [Lancers comparison](https://www.lancers.jp/c/website/website-knowhow/2851/)

### Inferences
- Selling Japanese/Korean customization at $249–449 means competing with ¥15k–50k Japanese freelancers who speak native Japanese. It is only viable for niches where multilingual output (JA + EN + ZH + KO) is the value: inbound-tourism B&Bs, salons, clinics and restaurants. There, ¥40k–80k (about $270–530) for a 4–5 language site is defensible.
- Suggested template price points: Japan ¥2,980–4,980 single, ¥14,800–29,800 bundle. Korea ₩29,000–49,000 single. (My inference from the comparables above.)

## 4. Competition and what top sellers offer

### Takeaway
Japan's Coconala LP category is crowded: hundreds of listings, many with zero sales, competing on speed, price and "unlimited revisions". Korea's Kmong is dominated by imweb/Cafe24 builders and Prime sellers offering fast delivery and packages. Template sellers in both markets bundle **education (e-books, VOD), setup help and ownership transfer**, not bare files.

### Cited Findings
- Coconala LP: about 70% of ¥10–30k listings have zero sales, and 40% have reviews (unofficial) — [Coconala blog](https://coconala.com/blogs/6165551/788154)
- Coconala listings differ on free revisions (2 vs unlimited) and on scope (SEO, contact form, copywriting) — [Coconala magazine 151](https://coconala.com/magazine/151)
- Kmong: imweb template plus e-book plus setup "full package" tiers. STANDARD (e-book + contract + prompts) vs DELUXE (+15 VOD lectures). Prices rise with each update — [Kmong 809166](https://kmong.com/gig/809166); [Kmong 557746](https://kmong.com/gig/557746)
- Kmong Prime (top 2%) LP sellers; one-page turnaround of 1–3 days vs 14 days for some — [Kmong 772452](https://kmong.com/gig/772452); [Kmong 790453](https://kmong.com/gig/790453)
- Digital-content refund rule common on Kmong: cancellation is possible before delivery starts, then limited by content type — [Kmong 727282](https://kmong.com/gig/727282)

### Inferences
- To stand out, a JA/KO version should come with a "how to edit/publish (Netlify/GitHub Pages/XServer)" guide in the local language, LINE/Kakao buttons pre-wired, and a commercial license for freelancers.

## 5. Legal / tax / compliance

### Takeaway
**Consumption tax / VAT is the biggest structural issue, and Gumroad, as merchant of record since Jan 2025, largely removes it for template sales made through Gumroad.** Selling direct (Stripe/PayPal) to Japanese consumers falls under Japan's "consumer-facing telecommunications services" regime: the foreign seller is liable, but the small-business exemption is reported at ¥10M. Korea requires simplified VAT registration within 20 days with **no threshold** under the NTS regime (one secondary source claims an ₩80M threshold, which conflicts). 特商法 disclosure applies to mail-order sales to Japanese consumers. Korea's new domestic-agent duty under the E-Commerce Act (from 2027-01-21) only hits large sellers (₩1T revenue / 1M users).

### Cited Findings
**Japan consumption tax**
- For consumer-facing (B2C) electronic services such as e-books and music distribution, **the foreign business that provides the service files and pays** — [NTA cross-border pamphlet](https://www.nta.go.jp/publication/pamph/pdf/cross-kokugai.pdf); [NTA Q&A](https://www.nta.go.jp/publication/pamph/pdf/cross-QA.pdf)
- Foreign businesses are also covered by the small-business exemption (事業者免税点制度) — [NTA pamphlet](https://www.nta.go.jp/publication/pamph/pdf/0024003-087_02.pdf). A third-party guide gives the threshold as ¥10M — [Fungies](https://fungies.io/global-tax-regulations-for-digital-products-and-subscriptions.md) (competitor marketing source; the ¥10M base-period sales rule is standard but not confirmed from the NTA in this session)
- Japanese business buyers can only deduct input tax on B2C services bought from **registered foreign businesses** (登録国外事業者), and the registration number must appear on books and invoices — [NTA cross-border Q&A](https://www.nta.go.jp/publication/pamph/pdf/cross-QA.pdf)
- **Platform taxation (from 2025-04-01):** when a foreign business supplies B2C electronic services through a designated platform (one receiving over ¥5B a year from foreign businesses), **the platform is deemed the supplier and files/pays**. It does not apply outside platforms — [NTA Tax Answer 6568](https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shohi/6568.htm); [KPMG 2024-12](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/jp/pdf/2024/jp-e-taxnews-20241227-1.pdf)
- Invoice system: relevant only if Japanese business buyers want to deduct tax. As an unregistered small foreign seller, you simply cannot issue qualified invoices. Japanese corporate clients (e.g. a clinic company buying customization) may prefer registered sellers, a minor competitive disadvantage — inference from the NTA Q&A above

**Korea VAT**
- Since 2015, foreign suppliers of electronic services to Korean consumers must register through **simplified business registration (간편사업자등록) within 20 days of starting business, file quarterly via Hometax, with no revenue threshold**. B2B is reportedly excluded (one law Q&A disagrees) — summary of [Forvis Mazars KR](https://www.forvismazars.com/kr/ko/insights/5/ai-saas); [NTS page](https://nts.go.kr/nts/na/ntt/selectNttInfo.do?nttSn=1342960); dissent: [albup](https://albup.co.kr/page/interpretation_detail.php?com_idx=16890)
- **Conflict:** Fungies (secondary) lists an ₩80M threshold for non-residents in Korea, which contradicts the "no threshold" statement above — [Fungies](https://fungies.io/global-tax-regulations-for-digital-products-and-subscriptions.md). Treat it as unresolved and verify against VAT Act Art. 53-2.

**Gumroad**
- Gumroad has been merchant of record since Jan 2025 and "will automatically handle all sales tax collection and remittance worldwide", collecting where it has obligations — [Gumroad taxes](https://gumroad.com/taxes); [Gumroad help: sales tax](https://gumroad.com/help/sales-tax)
- The search snippet did not show whether Japan and Korea are explicitly in Gumroad's collection list (gap).

**Japan 特定商取引法 (Specified Commercial Transactions Act)**
- Mail-order (通信販売) disclosure must include the seller's name, address and phone number, among other items; the 2022 amendment added final-confirmation-screen rules — [Cabinet Office/CAA material](https://www.cao.go.jp/consumer/kabusoshiki/digital/doc/007_220620_siryou1.pdf); [Japan Post column](https://www.post.japanpost.jp/bizpost/column/sales_promotion12/commerce-law/)
- Individuals may write "disclosed without delay upon request" for phone (and address, per CAA guidance) on the 特商法 page (Stripe's guidance, written for domestic sellers) — [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/how-to-create-and-display-a-commerce-disclosure-page?locale=ja-JP)
- The CAA has highlighted cases where it could not trace overseas sellers that violated the law, which shows enforcement concern about foreign sellers with unclear contact details — [CAA material (via search summary)](https://www.cao.go.jp/consumer/kabusoshiki/digital/doc/007_220620_siryou1.pdf)
- Stripe Japan requires a 特商法 page for Japanese merchants. Whether a Taiwan-based merchant selling via Gumroad (merchant of record) needs one is unclear: with Gumroad as merchant of record, the "seller" in the transaction is arguably Gumroad (inference, unverified)

**Japan APPI**
- APPI applies extraterritorially to foreign businesses that handle personal information of people in Japan in connection with supplying goods or services to them, including security-control and breach-reporting duties. Since the 2020 amendment, the PPC can require reports and issue orders against foreign businesses. No GDPR-style representative requirement — [PPC FAQ 11-3](https://www.ppc.go.jp/all_faq_index/faq1-q11-3); [PPC FAQ 11-2](https://www.ppc.go.jp/all_faq_index/faq1-q11-2); [BusinessLawyers](https://www.businesslawyers.jp/practices/615)

**Korea PIPA / E-Commerce Act**
- PIPA domestic-representative rules were tightened by an enforcement-decree amendment approved 2025-09-16, effective 2025-10-02: a foreign business must designate its Korean subsidiary if it has one, and must train and monitor the representative yearly — [Byline Network](https://byline.network/2025/09/16-554/); [Boannews](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=137479)
- E-Commerce Act amendment (passed 2025-12-30, promulgated 2026-01-20): **domestic-agent duty for foreign sellers with prior-year revenue ≥ ₩1T, or ≥ 1M monthly Korean visitors averaged over 3 months, or when the FTC requests data**. Effective **2027-01-21**; non-compliance can bring 3–12 month business suspensions. The decree was finalized 2026-07-14 (final thresholds should be confirmed) — [Law Times](https://www.lawtimes.co.kr/news/articleViewAmp.html?idxno=218003); [KDI EIEC](https://eiec.kdi.re.kr/policy/materialView.do?num=284229)
- Kmong requires 사업자등록 plus 통신판매업 신고 once conditions are met — [Kmong 708](https://kmong.com/support/expert-center/posts/708)

### Inferences
- **Gumroad as merchant of record is the key compliance shortcut.** Keep template sales there rather than moving to Stripe direct. Direct Stripe/PayPal sales to Korean consumers would technically trigger Korean simplified VAT registration from the first won (if the no-threshold reading is right).
- For **customization services** sold direct to Japanese/Korean businesses (B2B), the consumer-tax regimes mostly don't bite: Japan's reverse charge applies to business-facing services, and Korea reportedly excludes B2B.
- Contact forms in the templates: the **site owner** (the JP/KR SMB) is the data controller under APPI/PIPA, not the template seller. The seller should ship a privacy-policy page placeholder (個人情報保護方針 / 개인정보처리방침) and, for Japan, a 特商法表記 placeholder for shops that sell online. Korean sites need 개인정보처리방침 and, for sellers, the 사업자정보 footer (business name, CEO, registration number, 통신판매업 number), which Korean templates conventionally show. If the seller hosts form backends (e.g. Formspree under their own account) for clients, the seller becomes a processor and the analysis changes.
- The large-seller thresholds (₩1T, 1M users) make Korea's domestic-agent rules irrelevant to this seller.

### Gaps
- Exact current Korean VAT threshold for foreign electronic service suppliers (conflicting sources).
- Whether Gumroad collects Japanese consumption tax and Korean VAT (needs the Gumroad help page).
- Whether a foreign digital seller must post a Japanese address/phone for 特商法, and whether a Taiwan address is acceptable (no CAA guidance found specific to foreign sellers).
- PIPA domestic-representative thresholds (believed to be global revenue ≥ ₩1T, Korean services revenue ≥ ₩10B, or ≥ 1M Korean users' data, but not confirmed from a source in this session).

## 6. Localization needs beyond translation

### Takeaway
Japanese and Korean versions need more than text swaps: CJK typography (Noto Sans JP / Pretendard), denser information layouts, LINE vs KakaoTalk/Naver buttons, Naver/Kakao maps in Korea (Google Maps is weak there), local address formats, and legally conventional pages (特商法表記, privacy policy, Korean business-info footer).

### Cited Findings
- LINE: 100M monthly users in Japan at end of 2025. It is the de facto business messaging channel (LINE Official Account) — [LY Corp](https://www.lycorp.co.jp/ja/story/20260210/line_100million.html)

### Inferences (not sourced; practitioner knowledge)
- Fonts: Noto Sans JP / Noto Serif JP / M PLUS / Zen Kaku Gothic for JA. Pretendard (the de facto Korean web font) / Noto Sans KR / Spoqa Han Sans for KO. Subset or use Google Fonts to control CJK file sizes (a full CJK font is several MB).
- Typography: Japanese needs `line-break: strict`, `word-break: keep-all` for Korean (Korean wraps at spaces; Japanese doesn't use spaces) and wider line-height (1.7–1.9). Avoid italics. Text at the same font-size reads larger and denser than in Latin scripts.
- Design taste: Japanese SMB sites favour information density, explicit pricing tables (with tax-inclusive ¥ notation "税込"), access/parking info, business-hours tables, and "お問い合わせ" plus "予約" CTAs. Korean sites favour bold hero images, Instagram-style visuals, and footers with 사업자 info.
- Address formats: JP uses 〒123-4567 and goes prefecture → city → block. KR uses road-name address (도로명주소) plus 5-digit postcode, often with a Kakao/Daum postcode search widget on forms.
- Maps: Google Maps embeds are fine in Japan. In Korea, use Naver Map or Kakao Map links/embeds (Google Maps has limited Korean data due to map-export restrictions).
- Messaging buttons: LINE "友だち追加" QR/button (JP); KakaoTalk Channel "채널 추가/1:1 채팅" button and Naver TalkTalk (KR). Phone tel: links are critical in both.

## 8. Verdict inputs: revenue potential vs effort

### Takeaway
**Japan: a moderate, niche opportunity.** Add Japanese to the language switcher, sell via Gumroad plus BOOTH, and target web freelancers and inbound-tourism businesses (B&Bs, salons, clinics, restaurants serving foreign tourists). **Korea: low priority.** The ecosystem is Naver/Kakao/imweb-centric, local marketplaces are effectively closed to a Taiwan resident, and pure HTML has weak pull. Add Korean only as a cheap extra language in the switcher (helps Japan and Taiwan businesses that serve Korean tourists) rather than as a market entry.

### Cited Findings (summary of evidence above)
- Coconala is effectively inaccessible: it requires a JP phone, JP-issued ID and a JP bank account — [Coconala help](https://help.coconala.com/hc/ja/articles/218722417)
- BOOTH fees are low at 5.6% + ¥45 — [GameBusiness.jp](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2025/07/24/24711.html)
- JP LP service prices are ¥15k–50k typical, and templates about ¥3,000 — [Coconala magazine](https://coconala.com/magazine/151); [Coconala template](https://coconala.com/services/4240007)
- KR LP service prices are about ₩90k–190k — [Kmong 796249](https://kmong.com/gig/796249)

- Gumroad handles global sales tax as merchant of record — [Gumroad taxes](https://gumroad.com/taxes)

### Inferences
- Revenue potential (rough, unsourced): without local marketplaces or ads, organic JP/KR template sales via Gumroad/BOOTH likely run single-digit to low tens of sales per month at ¥3–5k each, i.e. **~$100–1,000/month** after a ramp-up of several months. Upside depends on SEO/X (Twitter)/note.com content in Japanese. The service side in Japan at $270–530 per multilingual site could add a few jobs a month if the inbound-tourism niche is marketed (e.g. through Japanese B&B/ryokan communities).
- Effort is about 1–2 weeks per language plus native review cost, which is low relative to even modest sales, so adding **Japanese is likely positive ROI**. Korean is marginal unless it is a near-free byproduct.
- Key risks: Japanese native competition at low prices; 特商法 disclosure of a home address if selling direct; inability to use Coconala/Kmong; support burden in Japanese/Korean.
